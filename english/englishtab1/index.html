Complete the second sentence so it means the same as the first
Page 177 · Use the word in bold · 2–5 words
  • 1
    I don't think it is a good idea to lie to your parents.
    were If I were you, I would not lie to your parents.
  • 2
    Vince would like to go to New York if he had some time off work.
    wishes Vince wishes he had time off work to go to New York.
  • 3
    It's a shame you broke your leg. You could have come skiing with us.
    broken If you hadn't broken your leg, you could have come skiing with us.
  • 4
    There was a snowstorm and that's why the flight was delayed.
    would If there hadn't been a snowstorm, the flight would have left on time.
  • 5
    It's a pity that I missed the play. I would have really enjoyed it.
    wish I wish I hadn't missed the play. I would have really enjoyed it.

Portfolio — What happened when the policeman arrived?
Page 109 · Creative writing · 3 variants to choose from
Variant A — The policeman sympathises with Oliver
When the policeman arrived at the workhouse, the hall was in uproar. The master stood red-faced and trembling, pointing at Oliver as though the boy had committed a terrible crime. Oliver himself was very still, his empty bowl hanging from his thin fingers, his eyes wide with fear. The policeman looked around the large stone hall — at the hollow-cheeked boys on their benches, at the copper barely big enough to feed them, at the cold stone walls — and a heaviness settled in his chest. He listened to the master's complaint in silence, then crouched down to Oliver's level and asked him quietly why he had asked for more. Oliver answered honestly: the boys were starving, he said, and simply couldn't help it. The policeman straightened up and turned to the master. He would make a note of the incident, he said, but he added — in a low voice — that the board of governors ought to be informed about conditions in the workhouse. He advised the master not to punish the boy too harshly. Then he put on his hat and left, and as the heavy door swung shut behind him, Oliver let out a long, shaking breath.
Variant B — The policeman follows the rules strictly
The policeman arrived at a run, his lantern swinging. He found the master barely in control of himself — shouting, gesturing, while the assistants held a small pale boy by the arms. The other boys sat rigid on their benches, too frightened to breathe. The policeman straightened his coat and asked for calm. The master explained what had happened: a boy had dared to walk up after supper and demand more gruel. The policeman's expression did not change. He took out his notebook and wrote something in it. Then he looked at Oliver and told him, with neither cruelty nor kindness, that rules in a workhouse existed for a reason and that breaking them had consequences. He helped the master escort Oliver to a small locked room at the back of the building. As he prepared to leave, one of the younger boys raised his hand and asked, in a very small voice, whether anyone would be bringing them water. The policeman paused. He told the master to see to it. Then he walked out into the dark street, and for a long time he did not look back.
Variant C — The policeman launches an investigation
The policeman did not hurry. He came in slowly, looked around the workhouse hall without speaking, and took off his hat. The master rushed forward with his complaint, but the policeman raised a hand and asked him to wait. He walked the length of the hall, studying the copper, peering into the empty bowls still stacked by the wall, noting the size of the room against the number of boys sitting in it. Several of the children watched him with large, hungry eyes. He stopped in front of Oliver, who stood apart from the others, and asked him his name. Oliver told him. The policeman asked what he had said to the master. Oliver repeated it: "Please, sir, I want some more." The policeman was quiet for a moment. Then he turned and told the master he would need to see the weekly ledger — the records of how much food was purchased for the workhouse versus how many boys were housed there.
